## 1. Bugs fixed — these were blocking a ship

### Payment confirmation was completely broken

`server/app/routes_bookings.py`

A refactor that extracted `settle_payment()` so the care link could share it
left the `@router.post("/payments/{payment_id}/confirm")` decorator behind on
the *helper*. Two consequences:

- The registered route had `db`, `payment` and `appt` as its parameters — a
  database handle and two sqlite rows, which FastAPI treated as required query
  strings, so every confirm returned 422.
- `confirm_payment()`, the real endpoint, was never routed at all. It also had
  ~50 lines of dead code after its `return`, left over from the same refactor.

**Nobody could complete a booking.** No appointment ever reached `confirmed`,
no Google Meet link was ever generated, and the flow dead-ended at the payment
sheet. Verified fixed end to end: book → pay → `confirmed` + meet link.

### "Continue to Pay" was unreachable, and tapping it fired the SOS alert

`src/screens/BookAppointment.jsx`, `src/index.css`

The booking screen's fixed footer sat at `bottom: 0`, but the tab bar is a real
element in the shell's flex column. Measured in Chrome at 390×844: the button
was **96% covered by the tab bar, and `elementFromPoint` at its centre returned
the SOS orb** — so the tap meant to pay for a consultation raised an emergency
alert instead.

Its padding also used `env(safe-area-inset-top)` for a *bottom* inset, leaving
nothing clear of the home indicator on a notched phone.

Replaced with a shared `.action-footer` class that sits above the tab bar
inside the shell and at `bottom: 0` outside it. Re-measured: 0% covered, centre
tap hits the button. Used by the booking screen, both intake forms, the
insurance editor and the claim receipt.

### `--sp-24` and `--sp-20` were used but never defined

`src/index.css`

Six screens end in `padding: … var(--sp-24)` to clear a fixed footer —
BookAppointment, DoctorProfile, DoctorRecordEditor and the new intake forms. An
undefined custom property makes the **whole shorthand invalid**, so those
screens rendered with no padding at all: content flush to both edges, last
field under the submit button. Added both tokens on the existing interpolation
(0.8× at 320px).

### Smaller

- **Seeding was all-or-nothing** (`server/app/db.py`) — `if COUNT(*) == 0`,
  which meant adding practitioners to `SEED_DOCTORS` did nothing on any
  database that had already booted, including every deployed one. Now matches
  per row by name, so a new specialty appears on the next restart and any
  doctor an admin edited is left alone.
- **`currentDoctor` was lost on refresh** (`BookAppointment.jsx`) — it lives in
  React context, which a reload empties. The screen rendered "Doctor ·
  Specialist", priced a chat consult at ₹0, and silently dropped both the
  anonymity notice and the new intake prompt, since both key off the specialty.
  It now fetches the doctor when context is empty.

## 2. Consultation intake forms (homeopathy + ayurveda)

Hema mam's case-taking proforma, built as a real form.

**`src/data/intake.js`** — both form specs as data.

- **Homeopathy**, 7 parts, in the classical case-taking order the proforma
  uses: chief complaint (onset / duration / progress / aggravation /
  amelioration) → concomitants and alternating complaints → past history
  (mumps, measles, jaundice, typhoid, hepatitis, hernia…) → family history →
  personal history (diet, appetite, thirst, urine, bowels, desires, aversions,
  thermals, sweat, sleep) → mind → difficult experiences. The order is not
  cosmetic; a repertorisation is worked up in it.
- Two additions to the proforma for this product: **menstrual history** in the
  personal section, and the classical **consolation** rubric ("when someone
  comforts you while you are upset, it — helps / makes it worse") in the mind
  section.
- **Ayurveda**, 5 parts: concern → prakriti (8 constitutional questions,
  dosha-weighted) → agni & koshta → dinacharya → artava. Submitting scores a
  **prakriti sketch** — vata/pitta/kapha percentages, dual-dosha when the top
  two are within one answer, and framed as a sketch a physician confirms rather
  than a result, because eight questions is not a diagnosis.

**How the trauma section is handled.** Hema mam's proforma asks about childhood
trauma, abuse, workplace humiliation, ragging and bullying, and marks the mind
section as the most crucial part. That is correct homeopathy and it is now in
the form — but it is the only place in Amruni that asks a woman about abuse, so
it is not sprung on her between appetite and sleep:

1. It is a **separate gated section** behind a consent card that says what is
   behind it, why a homeopath asks, who reads it, and that skipping costs her
   nothing. Skip and continue have equal visual weight.
2. **Its answers are never written to localStorage.** The rest of the form
   autosaves as she types; a woman's account of being abused as a child is not
   something to leave in a browser store on a shared family phone. Verified in
   a real browser: an ordinary answer lands in the draft and survives a reload,
   a sensitive one never appears in it. She is told this on the gate.
3. If she reports abuse, or that something still reaches her "a great deal",
   the confirmation screen **offers Tele-MANAS (14416)** — offered, in ordinary
   language, without treating what she wrote as an emergency she did not
   declare.

Also: sectioned one-part-per-screen with a tappable progress rail, autosaved
drafts, every question skippable, per-section required-field validation.

Files: `data/intake.js`, `hooks/useIntakeForm.js`, `screens/IntakeForm.jsx`,
`components/intake/{IntakeField,SensitiveGate,IntakeResult}.jsx`,
`services/intakeApi.js`.

Backend: `intake_submissions` table (**append-only** — she can refill before a
later consultation and the practitioner who saw her in March still reads what
she said in March), `routes_intake.py`, answers **encrypted at rest**.

## 3. Ayurveda, Reiki and Yoga

**`src/data/therapies.js`** + `/therapies` hub and `/therapies/:id` detail
pages, reachable from Home, Settings and the consult filter.

Each therapy carries an **evidence badge shown on the hub itself**, not buried
one level down:

- **Yoga — "Trial-supported."** Genuinely the best-evidenced thing here.
- **Ayurveda — "Recognised system."** Regulated by the Ministry of Ayush,
  BAMS-registered practitioners; evidence varies by preparation.
- **Reiki — "Complementary — not treatment."** It says plainly that controlled
  trials have not shown reiki to affect the course of any disease, and that it
  is offered as a relaxation practice. **This is a deliberate call and worth
  flagging to Hema mam.** A page promising reiki treats illness would cost the
  app every other clinical claim it makes, to the woman who later finds out.

Each page also carries a `caution` block **above** the booking button, not as a
footnote — the ayurveda one is about herb-drug interactions with blood
thinners, thyroid and diabetes medication, which is only useful before booking.

Links go to Ministry of Ayush, CCRAS, NCISM, MDNIY and the Yoga Certification
Board — government and council sources only, no affiliates. Reiki has no
equivalent body and so has no links, rather than padded ones.

Yoga has six practice tracks (prenatal, post-partum, cycle-synced, PCOS,
menopause, gentle/elderly). Five practitioners seeded across the three
disciplines, with registration/certification shown on the card.

## 4. Insurance capture (patients outside India)

**The honest framing, and the one thing to confirm with Hema mam.** Amruni does
not bill an insurer and does not settle a claim. She pays, we produce a
claim-ready receipt, she claims it back. Every string in the UI says so,
because telling a woman in Dubai her insurance is "accepted" and then handing
her a bill is worse than not offering the feature. If direct/cashless billing
is what the doctors meant, that is a payer-integration project and a different
piece of work.

- `/insurance` — country of residence (17 markets), payer type (private /
  employer / government / self-pay), insurer, plan, policy number, member ID,
  group number, TPA, policy holder + relationship, validity. Fields shown are
  driven by payer type; Indian government schemes autocomplete (Ayushman
  Bharat, CGHS, ESIC). Expiry warnings before she books, not after.
- **Numbers are masked to four digits everywhere except the receipt.** A policy
  number is the credential an insurer answers to over the phone. The doctor's
  chart shows `Daman · •••• 4567` and the API cannot return more to a doctor.
- **Booking screen** shows who is paying, and warns international patients to
  check that their policy covers overseas teleconsultations.
- **`/receipt/:appointmentId`** — the claim document. Practitioner,
  qualification, date, mode, diagnosis, amount, payment reference, full policy
  details, disclaimer. **Assembled entirely server-side** — a receipt is only
  worth anything to an insurer if the person submitting it could not have
  written it. Shares via the native share sheet, falls back to clipboard.
  Refuses to issue before payment (409), and **honours an anonymous booking**
  by leaving the name off rather than restoring it.

Files: `lib/insurance.js`, `screens/{Insurance,ClaimReceipt}.jsx`,
`services/insuranceApi.js`, `routes_insurance.py`, `insurance_policies` table
(all identifying columns encrypted).
